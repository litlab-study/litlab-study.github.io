Hope

Quote: "Maybe there is a beast ... maybe it's only us"


- The repetition of the adverb "maybe" introduces uncertainty and hesitation. This may reflect the fragility of hope on the island. His uncertainty may also highlight his youthful and innocent nature, making this realisation more intimate and evoking more sympathy from the reader.
- The time between the two halves of this quote also encourages the reader to dwell on it before the conclusion lands. This also amplifies tension
- The simple language mirrors the brutal clarity of Simon's insight. Also, as Simon is the allegorical Christ figure, this quote would have landed even more deeply for the pious audience.
(Side note - if you mention a character to be an "allerogrical __ figure", add a sentence or clause VERY briefly explaining how. For example; Simon is presented as a Messianic figure through his bond with nature, generosity, and when he "found for them the fruit they could not reach")
- On the surface, this shows a lack of hope as there is no chance of escaping such deep-rooted evil. However, their childlike ignorance and naive nature is being replaced by self-awareness, showsing hope of salvation through the confrontation of their inner darkness.

Quote: "The fire is the most important thing on the island. How can we ever be rescued except by luck, if we don't keep a fire going?"


- Ralph's constructive leadership is representative of hope, as he recognises that without cooperation, all that's left is chaos.
- The superlative "most" allows Ralph to elevate the fire above everything else and make it a symbol of hope. This shows he values civilisation and may present his leadership as a source of hope for survival.
- Overall, the fire is a symbol of hope. When the fire was neglected and went out, their civilisation also began to crumble as they lost their connection to the outside world.

Quote: "The conch exploded into a thousand white fragments and ceased to exist"


- This quote marks a complete collapse of any hope for order and civilisation.
- The conch was consistently used as a symbol of democracy and order - it provided hope for civilisation and gave power to speech and structure.
- The violent verb "exploded" is dramatic and sudden, encapsulating how grave the end of civilisation will be for humanity. Any hope for order is completely annihilated.
- The verb "ceased" presents finality but also implies a process. The conch tried to persist but savagery and evil were too overpowering.
- The hyperbole through "a thousand" emphasises the complete destruction - it has been shattered beyond repair.
- The adjective "white" was previously used to represent purity and peace, but Golding warns the readers that our innate human evil will leave all of these qualities destroyed.

Quote: "Ralph wept for the end of innocence and the darkness of man's heart"


- The verb "wept" shows deep emotional pain - he mourns the loss of morality, hope, and human goodness.
- The "end of innocence" suggests a loss of his childhood purity. His awareness of human capabilities has introduced a darker aspect to his life.
- Ralph is now no longer a naive schoolboy, he understands that the real threat was their capacity for evil when the restraints on society are removed.
- The noun "darkness" represents the moral corruption within them all. This encapsulates Golding's message that civilisation merely masks this darkness, so when law and order fall away, the darkness takes over.
- This represents a complete loss of hope in all of mankind. Golding shows that even with the return of civilisation (through the officer) the moral damage is irreversible.

Savagery

Quote: "Roger sharpened a stick at both ends"


- Roger is one of the most sadistic and violent characters, he enjoys inflicting pain and represents unfiltered cruelty
- The verb "sharpened" implies complete intent, suggesting a lack of hesitation and morality. As he plans to mount Ralph's head to the stick, this shows that they no longer view each other as humans but rather as prey.
- The noun "stick" was previously a tool used for survival, but is now used as a weapon. This demonstrates their descent into savagery and the moral corruption brought about by human beings.

Quote: "Right up her ass!"


- The adverbial phrase "right up" is violently specific, implying force and total domination. It adds to the graphic cruelty of the scene an demonstrates their whole-hearted intent.
- The feminine pronoun "her" adds a layer of sexual misuse, making it deeply disturbing and showing their desperate desire for violence and control.
- The coarse and colloqual language through "ass" amplifies their loss of innocence. Instead of just a kill, it has become a mockery and act of cruel performance. This may reflect how this was a form of entertainment to them, revealing a lack of concern for morality.
- This quotes brings forth misogynistic aspects, further showing the deterioration of their decorum and savage inclinations. As female characters were still seen as inferior and weaker at the time, their choice to hunt a female pig mirrors the societal oppression towards women. They targeted the most vulnerable prey as their aim was not just to eat, but also to assert dominance and seize control.

Quote: "Roger, with a sense of delirious abandonment, leaned all his weight onto the lever"


- The adjective "delirious" implies ecstasy and a lack of control, violence is now pleasurable and liberating to Roger. Savagery brings him to a trance-like, blissfull state, perhaps mirroring addiction.
- The noun "abandonment" shows that he has completely rejected the morality of civilisation.
- The determiner "all" suggest full commitment with no hesitation, showing the extent of his innate savagery.
- The noun "lever" also distances Roger from the act as he uses indirect violence, making it easier to kill without remorse. This could also be seen as a metaphor for modern warfare. Golding criticises how technology (such as atomic bombs) makes killing easier and more impersonal.
- His emotional absence and delirious pleasure show the complete collapse of morality.

Quote: "Kill the pig. Cut her throat. Spill her blood"


- The imperative "kill" immediately introduces violence and finality. There is no moral hesitation and it shows an emergence of brutal instincts.
- The noun "pig" may be used to represent innocence and helplessness - and it may also forshadow Piggy's murder.
- The pronoun "her" adds a gendered dimension and reveals underlying themes of misogyny and dominance over the feminine. At the time, women were still seen as inferior so this reveals how the boys prey on the weak to claim more power.
- The verb "spill" and noun "blood" may also give the act a ceremonial quality and allows the boys to exercise their control.
- The simple, monosyllabic structure of the quote juxtaposes their brutal intentions. This may act as a reminder of their youth and allows Golding to convey his message about innate human evil. It may also give the chant a hypnotic quality - showing how they are all completely brainwashed by their desire for power.
- Overall, it shows the breakdown of order and the rise of primal instincts.

Quote: "There were no words, and no movements but the tearing of teeth and claws"


- The noun "words" may be used to represent civilisation - when the boys lose language, they lose their moral and social identity. They are no longer rational individuals- they've become a mindless and savage mob.
- The absence of language shows a complete breakdown of communication, reason, and humanity - they are so overmastered by their violent instincts that they abandon all humanity.
- The violent verb "tearing" conveys ongoing and uncotrolled brutality. Their lack of clear "movements" may present them as a threatening, unified mob with a complete lack of human grace.
- Zoomorphism is used through the metaphorical nouns "teeth and claws". Their fear has driven them to become what they feared as they embody a beast-like behaviour. This directly links to the novel's central idea - that the real evil is within humans themselves.
- This moment shows their innate savagery as it was not planned - it was purely instinctive.

Quote: "The desire to squeeze and hurt was overmastering"


- The verb "squeeze" connotes pain and control while the blunt verb "hurt" shows their brutal intentions.
- The noun "desire" represents a deep, emotional or instinctual craving. Golding suggests that violence is seductive because of our nature.
- The adjectve "overmastering" shows that Ralph is not in control of his violent urge. This suggests that our innate savagery is stronger than our willpower and morality.
- Ralph was previously one of the more reasonable characters, however this shows civilisation's grip on him begin to weaken.
