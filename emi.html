The Emigree

Summary Key quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

Carol Rumens explores a speaker's positive memories of a homeland she was forced to leave as a child. Despite political changes and conflict, she continues to view her country with affection and nostalgia. The poem explores themes of memory, identity, and belonging.

Key quotes

"It may be sick with tyrants, but I am branded by an impression of sunlight"

"That child's vocabulary I carried here like a hollow doll, opens and spills a grammar"

"They mutter death, and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight"

Analysed quotes

Quote: "It may be sick with tyrants"
The modal verb "may" introdcues uncertainty. She knows the country has changed, but is unwilling to accept it fully. She's in denial because she yearns for the comfort of her home and she grieves her country before it got ill.
The adjective "sick" connotes corruption - this powerful metaphor illustrates her country's political decay.
The noun "tyrants" creates a stark contrast with the beauty and purity of her childhood memories (shown through the symbolism of "sunlight"). It reinforces themes of oppression and identity loss.
The verb "branded" implies she is permanently marked with love for her country. This may also build her internal conflict - branding is usually violent, but it is linked to her warm memories. However, the noun "impression" may suggest these memories are vague or fading as the country is destroyed.
Encapsulates the speaker's emotional entrapment- the memory of her homeland has left a permanent psychological mark. The semantic field of memory illustrates the struggle to hold onto a vague memory of a place that no longer exists.

Quote: "They mutter death, and my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight"
The vague pronoun "they" creates ambiguity as it could mean the people in her new country or the oppressive political figures in her old one.
The verb "mutter" is secretive, reflecting the subtle acts of prejudice or discrimination she faces in her new country.
The noun "death" could be literal (war or persecution) or symbolic of the death of her identity that was bound to her past country.
Throughout the poem, "sunlight" was a symbol of hope, however it is now representative of the past. The quote suggests that due to the people in her new country tourmenting her, she has accepted the fate of her own country.
In the final line, Rumens reinforces the poem's central tension between loss and the persistence of love through the juxtaposition between "sunlight" and "shadow". She now desperately tries to cling to any evidence that there ever even was sunlight.

Context

Although the poem is not based on one specific country, it reflects the experiences of people who have been forced to leave their homeland because of war, conflict, or political oppression. Rumens explores how memories can shape a person's identity and preserve a connection to a place even after they have left it.
