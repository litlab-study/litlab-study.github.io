Storm on the Island

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

Seamus Heaney describes a small island community preparing for and experiencing a powerful storm. The poem explores the overwhelming power of nature and how humans can feel vulnerable and helpless against forces they cannot control.

Quotes

"We are prepared: we build our houses squat"

"There are no trees, no natural shelter"

"Exploding comfotably down on the cliffs"

"Spits like a tame cat turned savage"

"We are bombarded by the empty air. Strange, it is a huge nothing that we fear."

Analysed quotes

Quote: "We are prepared: we build our houses squat"
The collective pronoun "we" creates a sense of unity within the community - they are united against a common enemy.
The declarative format shows their confidence, perhaps showing how accustomed they have become to the "storm".
The adjective "squat" may show discomfort, showing how their entire lives have been compromised due to the conflict. This may link to the bombs that were planted during the troubles, which made it extra difficult to go about daily life.

Context

The poem is written about the religious conflicts between the Catholics and Protents in Ireland.
The storm could be literal or a metaphor for the constant political disturbances.

Form and structure

The poem is composed of one long stanza with many long, winding sentences - reflects the uncontrollable and overwhelming aspects of both nature and the northern Irish conflict.
The dramatic monologue format may be used to reflect the isolation of the islanders who got no help.
The collective pronoun "we" creates a sense of collective experience, emphasising the intensity of the effects of the conflcit as everyone experiences the same fear and vulnerability.
