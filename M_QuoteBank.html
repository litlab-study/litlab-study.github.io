Macbeth

Quote: "Stars, hide your fires, let not light see my black and deep desires"


- This shows his internal conflict. Although he deeply desires power, he is not yet willing to openly challenge God by murdering the divinely appointed king.
- The celestial imagery "stars" may be symbolic of guidance, perhaps foreshadowing hoe Macbeth will go against his natural fate. Aternatively, the stars could also symbolise God, allowing Macbeth to address him directly and showing his deep religious guilt and fear.
- The juxtaposition between "light" and "black" may further emphasise Macbeth's internal conflict - this depicts the choice he must make between morality or uncontrolled ambition.

Quote: "Sleep no more, Macbeth does murder sleep"


- Sleep is often used as a symbol of innocence throughout the play. Here, sleep is personified, implying that Macbeth destroyed the innocence of him and his wife. From here, they also suffer from insomnia and their lack of sleep represents the relentless guilt that follows them.
- The noun "sleep" may also connote rest and renewal, so this quote shows the extent of the ramifications of regicide. He will be tourmented by the consequences of going against God for the rest of his life.
- Contextually, this clear display of the implications of regicide will have succesfully helped deterring the nobles in the audience from regicide.

Quote: "Out, out brief candle. Life's but a walking shadow".


- This quote shows Macbeth adopting a nihilistic view as he is worn by his guilt and paranoia. Shakespeare suggests that this is the consequence of going against God.

Gender roles

Quote: "Be innocent of the knowledge dearest chuck"


- Marks his descent into isolation. He considers his plan too brutal for her, showing the shifting power dynamic.
- The derogatory term "chuck" can be seen as him looking down upon her, showing a newfound superior attitude that contradicts his previous affection towards her.
- It may also juxtapose the term of endearment "dearest", emphasising his cunning manipulation.
- The noun "innocent" could alternatively seem more as though he is protecting her from his plans, conforming to the Jacobean views that women could not handle such violence.
- He has gone from more ideal for the modern audience (caring, appreciative, equal) to a more dominant character that abides by the expectations of the contemprary audience.

Quote: "Yet I do fear thy nature. It is too full o'th'milk of human kindness"


- This reveals that Macbeth is not naturally vicious despite his violent endeavours. However, Lady Macbeth criticises this and views it as weakness.
- The noun "milk" may connote femininity and nurture, perhaps stripping Macbeth of his masculinity.

QUote: "Unsex me here, and fill me from the crown to the toe top-full of direst cruelty"


- Lady Macbeth calls on the supernatural to strip her of her femininity, so she can take on the role of a cold-blooded killer.
- This illustrates the gender roles in this play, as she believes that in order to achieve any power, she must embody traits associated with masculinity. However, her cruel intentions were unnatural, which is why she must call on the supernatural to help her. It could also suggest that she is inable to handle the consequences of her unnatural deeds.

Quote: "Had he not resembled my father as he slept, I had done't"


- This quote may reveal a softer, more vulnerable side to her - as though she's incapable of violence. This confirms Jacobean stereotypes that women were too fragile to be violent.

Duncan

Kingship

Quote: "This dead butcher and his fiend-like queen"


- This shows the ultimate consequences of usurpation and unnatural kingship - they inevitably lose their status. There is no respect for either of them and they are both dehumanised, further warning the audience of the consequences of regicide.
- The possessive pronoun "his" further belittles Lady Macbeth as it reduces her to a sort of accessory to Macbeth's reign. This shows the Jacobean audience that going against the natural gender roles is futile and will not have any lasting effects.

Banquo

Supernatural

Quote: "Is this a dagger which I see before me?"


- This may be seen as a manifestation of the witches, who play with his "heat-oppressed brain" in order to drive him to commit regicide.
- The rhetorical question depicts his hesitation, showing he is not yet fully committed to the regicide.
- This is his first hallucination, foreshadowing his future hallucinations of Banquo's ghost which will haunt him for the rest of his life.

Deception

Quote: "Fair is foul and foul is fair"


- The witches immediately warn the audience not to trust appearances.
- The inversion is used to make the quote stand out and linger in the minds of the audience.
