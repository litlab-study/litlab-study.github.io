Summary

Imtiaz Dharker explores the power and fragility of paper as a symbol for human life, identity, and the structures that shape society. The poem suggests that although humans try to create permanent systems of control, true strength comes from openness, change, and human connection.

Quotes

"Paper that lets the light shine through, this is what could alter things."

"Maps too. The sun shines through their borderlines"

"With living tissue, raised a structure never meant to last"

Analysed quotes

Quote: "Paper that lets the light shine through, this is what could alter things"
The noun "light" could be symbolic for God - perhaps suggesting that people should allow God to work through and carry out his plans.
Alternatively, it could also be a metaphor for truth an honesty - implying that being more truthful to each other can lead to changes in the world.

Context

Dharker's work often explores themes of identity, culture, belonging, and the ways people are connected across different societies. In Tissue, paper symbolises human systems such as maps, money, books, and records, which people use to organise and control the world. The poem reflects modern concerns about power, borders, and the structures that govern society. Dharker suggests that these systems are temporary and fragile, while human life and relationships are ultimately more important.
