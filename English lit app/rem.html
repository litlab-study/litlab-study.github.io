Remains

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

This poem was written as part of a collection exploring the real psychological impact of war on soldiers, particularly modern conflicts. His aim was to show the emotional aftermath - the trauma and inescapable memories.

Quotes

"I see every round as it rips through his life"

"And the drink and drugs won't flush him out"

"he's here in my head when I close my eyes, dug in behing enemy lines"

"Not left for dead in some distant, sun-stunned, sand-smothered land"

"His bloody life in my bloody hands"

Analysed quotes

Quote: "Not left for dead in some distant, sun-stunned, sand-smothered land"
The vague determiner "some" shows the speaker trying to emotionally detatch himself from his actions. This could also reflect the incomprehensibility of modern war when soldiers are thrown into unknown land.
The compound adjectives ("sun-stunned", "sand-smothered") may reflect how the speaker is overwhelmed while also suggesting that the soldiers are besieged by natural forces.
The sibilance may create a hypnotic rhythm - like a memory loop that keeps haunting him.
This quote could also reflect the effect of the war on nature. Such beautiful, effulgent landscapes are turned into backdrops for personal nightmares.

Quote: "His bloody life in my bloody hands"
Through the pronoun "his", the speaker humanises the other soldier. He acknowledges that the man killed was a real person, not just a 'target'. The speaker takes responsibility for his actions.
The adjective "bloody" could mean that he is literally covered in blood - creating a vivid image of suffering. Alternatively, this could also be colloquial language - presenting frustration and distress. He is stained with guilt both emotionally and morally.
The metaphor shows moral responsibility. It suggests control, but ironically as he feels helpless. He's completely burdened by his actions.
This final line is an expression of trauma and survivor's guilt. Armitage shows how war leaves lasting psychological scars, blurring the line between patriotic defence and needless killing.
The poem intends to explore the real psychological impact of war on soldiers. This quote highlights the long-term psychological damage of war.

Context

The poem focuses on the psychological impact of warfare rather than the physical violence itself. It reflects growing awareness of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and the lasting effects that combat can have on soldiers long after they return home. Through the speaker's guilt and traumatic memories, Armitage highlights the emotional consequences of war and challenges romanticised views of military service.

Form and structure

The structure is reflective of the mental collapse and overpowering memories caused by war.
The final 2-line stanza breaks the pattern. This shows that the soldiers may have first experience the strict structure of war, but that has now faded.
The rest of the poem adheres to regular quatrains, creating the illusion of contained chaos. This illustrates that any bisible order during war is completely performative. The repetitive structure may also mimic how trauma replays in the mind. The free-verse and enjambment reflect the unpredictable and chaotic nature of trauma - there is absolutely no relief or escape.
