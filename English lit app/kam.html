Kamikaze

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

The narrator's father was sent to be a kamikaze pilot and the narrator is now re-telling his story. Her father didn't end up going through with the bombing and returns home. Back at home, he is ostracised and everyone has lost respect for him.

Quotes

1. "Her father embarked at sunrise"

2. "A shaven head full of powerful incantations"

3. "little fishing boats strung out like bunting on a green-blue translucent sea"

4. "he must have wondered which had been the better way to die"

Analysed quotes

1. The distant narrative creates detatchment and mey reflect the isolation experienced by the father once he returned.
The verb "embarked" suggests a journey. This is both literal (the kamikaze mission) and metaphorical (his emotional and moral journey).
The noun "sunrise" is symbolic of new beginnings and hope - this is ironic because of the dark nature of the mission. It may also link symbollically to Japan (known as 'the land of the rising sun'), reinforcing ideas of patriotism and national identity.
The quiet, calm image contrasts with the chaos and destruction of war. Thid may suggest that he began the mission with honourable intentions, but the rest of the poem reveals the cost of choosing life over honour. There's an underlying tension between duty and humanity that's present from the opening line.

3. The phrase "strung out" implies something delicately arranged - creating a visual image of beauty and fragility.
The simile using "bunting" connotes celebration, contrasting the grave nature of the kamikaze mission. This may carry a nostalgic tone as he perhaps reflects upon previous joyous moments of his life. This further reinforces the power of nature and memory as they are likely to have influenced his decision to turn back.
The adjectives "green-blue" and "translucent" evoke a sense of tranquillity, natural beauty and serenity - sharply contrasting the violent purpose of the journey. This depicts the power of nature as it's beauty comforts him and lures him away from his mission.

4. The modal verb "must" implies speculation, creating a tone of uncertainty and empathy. She has to imagine his internal conflict, as he is too isolated to tell her.
The stark final word "die" allows Garland to emphasise the inescapability of death. The poem does not close with resolution, but with lingering doubt and a sense of emotional tragedy. This stronly encapsulates the horrors of war and the moral dilemmas faced by those involved.
This final line powerfully captures the emotional and psychological cost of the pilot's decision to return home. Garland uses a rhetorical tone to emphasis the conflict between national duty and personal survival, suggesting that in some ways, choosing to live was the more painful path. This final reflection leaves the reader questioning the cost of honour, and whether survival is truly victory if it leads to isolation & shame.

Context

Being a kamikaze pilot was seen as very honourable because it meant that they were prepared to sacrifice their lives for their country. If a kamikaze pilot returned, they were seen as a coward and often punished.
