The Charge of the Light Brigade

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

Written about the Crimean war. The poem mainly praises the bravery an loyalty of the British soldiers, while also criticising the 'blunder' that led to their deaths.

Quotes

"Into the valley of Death rode the six hundred"

"Someone had blunder'd"

"Theirs not to reason why, Theirs but to do and die"

"All the world wonder'd"

"Storm'd at with shot and shell"

Quote analysis

Quote: "Into the Valley of Death rode the six hundred"
The 'valley of death' acts as biblical imagery - perhaps suggesting that God is on the soldiers' side and hinting at the novelty of their sacrifices. This links to the story of David and Golliath and the valley in which they fought - symbolising how the British were overpowered and abandoned by their leaders. This religious allusion and the phrase "the six hundred" is repeated throughout the poem, capturing the intensity of the event.

Quote: "Someone had blundered"
The indefinite pronoun "someone" is ambiguous and creates mystery, Tennyson makes a generalisation and critiques all aristocratic leaders.
This quote also doesn't conform to the dactylic dimeter, emphasising it. Tennyson is subtly drawing attention to his disgust towards the blunder that caused the fatalities. This abrupt break to the meter may also mirror the dramatic stop and premature shortening of the soldiers' lives.
The chess terminology may also make it seem like a game; as though the soldiers were mere pawns to be sacrificed. This criticises the indifference of the leaders.

Context

The poem focuses on parts of the battle of balaclava. Tennyson wrote about the miscommunications that led to many futile deaths of British soldiers as they charged straight into the Russian's fire.

Form and structure

The ballad form suggest that it's an awe-inspiring act of bravery that should be passed on to future generations. Ballads were traditionally sung, showing their importance as it made them more memorable. However, it also shows that we must never forget the mistakes made by the aristocratic leadership.

Dactylic dimeter (1 stressed syllable followed by 2 unstressed syllables) is used because Tennyson wants us to fully appreciate the bravery of the soldiers. It may mimic the galloping of the horses, immersing the reader into the poem and allowing them to truly empathise with the soldiers and appreciate them more. It is also sometimes reffered to as a 'falling rhythm', perhaps reflecting the losses due to the war and therefore further encapsulating the devastating result of the leaders' mistakes.
