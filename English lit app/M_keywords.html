Key words for Macbeth

Usurpation: the taking of a position of power or importance unlawfully or by force.

Regicide: the action of killing a king.

Anagnorisis: the point in a play, novel, etc., in which a principal character recognizes or discovers another character's true identity or the true nature of their own circumstances.

Hamartia: a fatal flaw or crucial error that leads to the downfall of a tragic hero or heroine.

Machiavellian villain/villainess: A person who schemes in a cunning and unscrupulous way. E.g. Lady Macbeth.

Aristotelian tragic hero: a noble character who experiences a downfall due to a tragic flaw (hamartia) or mistake.

Patronage: the support given by a patron (patronage)

Facade: a deceptive outward appearance.

Hubris: excessive pride or self-confidence.

Didactic: intended to teach, particularly in having moral instruction as an ulterior motive.

Pious: devoutly religious.

Emasculate: to deprive of strength or vigor; weaken.

Nihilism: the rejection of all religious and moral principles, often in the belief that life is meaningless.

Cautionary: serving as a warning.

Divine right of kings: the doctrine that kings derive their authority from God.

Great Chain of Being: a hierarchical structure of all matter and life, believed by medieval Christianity to have been decreed by God. It begins with God and descends through angels, humans, animals, and plants to minerals, representing a metaphysical order of existence.

Patriarchy: a social system in which men hold primary power and predominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, social privilege, and control of property.

Hegemonic: an adjective describing authority, dominance, or leadership exercised by one group, state, or idea over others.
