How are the effects of power shown in 'My Last Duchess' and one other poem?

How are the effects of war shown in 'Kamikaze' and one other poem?

In both ‘Kamikaze’ and ‘War Photographer’ the poets present the harrowing effects on soldiers, witnesses, and families of soldiers. Both poems also specifically detail the indifference and unempathetic responses from those who did not directly face the conflict, through the soldier’s family and society in general.

In Kamikaze, we are told from a third person perspective, the story of a Kamikaze pilot. Before the mission, he had a “shaven head full of powerful incantations”. This metaphor symbolises the oppression they faced and exposes the psychological manipulation they faced. The adjective “shaven” could present the father as a prisoner, showing how heavily he was controlled. This treatment of him juxtaposes his own intentions, as he was sent to sacrifice his own life for his country. The noun “incantations” may also allude to the propaganda they were indoctrinated with. Alternatively, it could also symbolise physical torture that was used to control his mind or perhaps the drugs that were given to soldiers sent on these missions. This presents the pilot as a mere pawn to the government, reflecting the inhumane treatment that they faced. Similarly, war photographer also uses a more distant narrative, perhaps suggesting that society discards soldiers and does not actually listen to their own stories. In ‘Kamikaze’, distance is created as the story has been passed down through generations without any of the soldiers’ input, while in ‘war photographer’, we hear only the perspective a photographer who struggles with how society erases the voices of soldiers also. The photographer knows that; “The reader's eyeballs prick with tears between the bath and pre-lunch beers”. Here, the verb “pray” connotes quick, distilled pain, emphasising the true lack of sympathy the readers experience. The nouns “bath” and “beers” may also encapsulate the luxury of their lives and how they are not affected by what they see at all. Therefore, both poems show the impacts of war on those who witness it and the lack of empathy from those who don’t.
