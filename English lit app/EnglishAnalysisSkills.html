Key language and structural techniques:


Noun: A person, place, thing, or idea.
Verb: An action or state.
Modal verb: Expresses necessity or possibility.
Imperative: Gives an order or command.
Adverb: Modifies a verb, adjective, phrase, or another adverb.
Adjective: Describes a noun.
Pronoun: Used in place of a noun.
Superlative: An adjective showing the highest quality or degree.
Symbolism: The use of symbols to represent ideas or qualities.
Alliteration: The same letter or sound at the start of adjacent words.
Metaphor: Direct comparison of two things without using 'like' or 'as'.
Personification: The giving of human characteristics to a non-human object.
Semantic field: The use of a group of words that all link to the same theme.
Simile: Comparing two things using 'like' or 'as'.
Hyperbole: Exaggeration that should not be taken literally.
Imagery: Vivid description of a particula scene.
Auditory imagery: Vivid description of sounds.
Tactile imagery: Vivid imagery of physical sensation.
Olfactory imagery: Vivid description of smell.
Gustatory imagery: Vivid description of taste/flavour.
Kinesthetic imagery: Vivid description of movement or body position.
Simple sentence: Contains one clause with a subject and verb.
Compound sentence: Contains two independent clauses that are related and joined with a conjunction.
Complex sentence: Contains one or more subordinate clause.
Repetition: A word or phrase that is used more than once.
Rule of three: 3 adjectives or phrases used to describe something.
Juxtaposition: Two things placed together to highlight the contrast between them.
Anaphora: The repetition of a word or phrase ar the beginning of successive clauses.
Asyndetic listing: Listing without the use of conjunctions (only using commas).
Polysndetic listing: Listing with the use of more than one conjunction.
Chronological order: Events arranged in the order they occured over time.
Analepsis: A flashback (set earlier than the main narrative).
Prolepsis: A flashforward (set later than the main narrative).
Exposition: Introduction of background information on characters, events or settings to help anchor the reader.
Climax: The most intense point in the developing of the narrative.
Foreshadowing: An advanced hint at what is to come later.
Oxymoron: When two contrasting words are placed directly beside each other.

Ambitious vocabulary:

Besieged: To be surrounded by hostile forces.
Callous: Cold or unfeeling.
Austere: Strictly disciplined, severely simple, or lacking in comfort.
Indolent: Habitually lazy.
Disconsolate: Deeply unhappy and without hope.
Enraptured: To be filled with intense joy.
Beguiled: To be charmed, captivated, or tricked into doing something.
Sycophant: One who flatters an authoritative figure for their own gain.
Sepharic: Angelic, sweet.
Euphonic: Pleasing to the ear.
Elated: To be extremely happy and excited.

Paper 2 question 5:

- Develop counterarguments. Try to acknowledge at least one but argue against it using evidence to support your own viewpoint.
- Don't use unrealistic claims! Keep statistics as realistic as possible.
- Keep an academic, authoritative tone.
- Think about each sentence and try to ensure they all serve a purpose (developing, challenging, supporting etc)
- End by reinforcing your arguement and try to leave the reader thinking.
- Use PRECISE vocabulary and a large range of punctuation (correctly).
