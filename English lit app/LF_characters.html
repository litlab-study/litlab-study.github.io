LoTF character analysis

Ralph:

- His dad is a commander in the navy, perhaps foreshadowing his future arrogance as he believes he knows more than the others. He clearly exercises his role as leader and asserts dominance from the beginning; "His voice was loud and savage, and struck them into silence".
- He clings to structure and civilisation; "We can't have everybody talking at once".
- He also takes his role seriously from the beginning and remains reasonable; "i'm calling an assembly".

Piggy:

- He is consistently marginalised and overlooked by the other boys ("The boys were a closed circuit with Piggy outside"). He is textually feminised and presented as a motherly figure as he cares for and defends the other boys.

Simon:

- "His eyes so bright they had deceived Ralph into thinking him delightfully gay and wicked"
- "Simon found for them the fruit they could not reach".
- Simon is consistenly presented as an allegorical Christ figure, portraying him as generally more mature and morally aware than the other boys.
- "Simon became inarticulate in his efforts to express mankind's essential illness"

Jack:

- He displays clear primitive instincts from the beginning; "He began to dance and his laughter became a bloodthirsty snarling".
- Contrary to Ralph, he does not try to cling to civilisation; "Jack clamoured amongst them, the conch forgotten".
