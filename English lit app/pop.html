Poppies

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

The main theme is about the bond between a mother and her son. It shows the caring nature of motherly love. It starts woth a mother preparing her son to go off to war. At the end, the mother is at a memorial site, but, as the reader, we don't now exactly why.

Quotes

1. "I pinned one to your lapel, crimped petals"

2. "the world overflowing like a treasure chest"

3. "I listened, hoping to hear your playground voice catching on the wind"

Analysed quotes

1. The verb "pinned" shows a delicate, caring, and almost ceremonial act. However, it also suggests being held in place or restricted - possibly reflecting how the son's fate is now sealed. Alternatively, it could also reflect how the mother now feels trapped due to her grief and cannot move on. This verb inittialy suggests a tender, maternal action - the speaker preparing her son with care. However, it can also hint at pain or violence (because pins pierce), potentially symbolising the emotional wounding she experiences.
The formal attire suggests a transition into adulthood and shows the significance of his duty. The level of detail also shows how closely she's connected to him, and how deeply the memory lives in her. The intimacy represents her maternal love.
The adjective "crimped" provides a visual of damage and imperfection. This illustrates how war distorts beauty and damages innocence. Alternatively, it could also mimic burnt or wounded skin, perhaps subtly foreshadowing her son's fate.
The imagery of petals is also significance as they connote softness and beauty, while also being very delicate - almost like life itself.

2. The noun "world" could represent the freedom and opportunity the boy sees ahead of him - he may see the war as an adventure or way to prove his courage. This contradics the mother's point of view, as she likely sees the dangers and unknown risks associated with war.
The verb "overflowing" on one hand suggests energy, optimism and a positive overwhelming feeling. The boy may be romanticising war - showing how young men may be lured into war by illusions of glory through propaganda. However, this couls also link the mother's emotional overflow. She experiences so many emotions at once, and it becomes chaotic and uncontrolled. This may also be a metaphor for her lack of control over her son's future - she is anxious of what he has ahead of him.
The childish imagery though "treasure chest" further represents the boy's optimism or feeling of duty.
The simile initially evokes a sense of excitement and possibility - perhaps from the son's perspective, the world is rich with opportunity. However, the mother may interpret this differently; the idea of a treasure chest could also symbolise loss, especially when overflowing suggests a lack of control or containment, mirroring her fear as she lets him go.

3. The verb "listened" shows grief and longing - she's actively searching for him, suggesting that she hasn't emotionally let go. She clings to the past, replaying her child's voice in her mind despite the damage done by the conflict.
The present participle "hoping" shows the hope is ongoing. She remains delusional and refusin to accept loss - portrays the psychologically captivating impacts of war.
There is stark juxtaposition between this childhood imagery and the current war. This crystal-clear memory haunts her and amplifies her grief.
The natural imagery of the wind shows ephemerality - his presence is fading. It also shows the inevitability of loss as wind continues to move no matter the obstacles around it.
The lack of closure and ambiguous ending in this line mirrors grief, leaving the reader with a sense of emptiness and fragility. This quote evokes the profound emotional impact of war and uses nature as a metaphor for memory - fleeting and intangible.

Context

This poem is a response to the losses already suffered during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. Jane Weir was Anglo-Italian and lived in Manchester. She mentions Armistice Sunday which was origionally for WW1 soldiers but is now used to remember all soldiers.

Form and structure

The dramatic monologue emphasises the intense pain caused to the mothers of soldiers. We also don't hear from the son at all, emphasising his absence and allowing the reader to see the loss faced by the mother.

The freeverse and enjambment may be used to encapsulste the overwhelming nature of her emotions and how her feelings are difficult to contain.

The use of varied stanza lengths and caesura may be used to mirror the chaos of war and the lack of control mother's have over their son's wellbeing.
