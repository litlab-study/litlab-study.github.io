Summary:

Macbeth is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare around 1606. The play follows the rise and fall of Macbeth, a brave Scottish soldier whose ambition leads him to commit terrible crimes.
At the beginning of the play, Macbeth and his friend Banquo meet three witches who prophesize that Macbeth will become King of Scotland. Encouraged by the prophecy and persuaded by his ambitious wife, Lady Macbeth, Macbeth murders King Duncan while he is a guest in their castle. Macbeth is crowned king, but he becomes increasingly paranoid and fearful of losing power.
To protect his position, Macbeth arranges the murder of Banquo after learning that Banquo's descendants are destined to become kings. Although Banquo is killed, his son Fleance escapes. Macbeth is haunted by guilt and begins to see visions, including Banquo’s ghost at a royal banquet.
Macbeth returns to the witches, who give him more prophecies. Believing himself to be invincible, he becomes even more ruthless and orders the murder of Macduff’s wife and children. Meanwhile, many Scottish nobles turn against him, and Duncan’s son Malcolm gathers an army to reclaim the throne.
As Macbeth’s enemies advance, Lady Macbeth is overwhelmed by guilt and eventually dies. In the final battle, Macbeth learns that the witches’ prophecies were misleading. Macduff, who was born by Caesarean section rather than natural birth, kills Macbeth. Malcolm becomes king, restoring order to Scotland.
The play explores themes such as ambition, power, guilt, the supernatural, appearance versus reality, and the consequences of unchecked desire. Shakespeare shows how ambition can corrupt individuals and lead to their downfall.

Context:

Macbeth was written by William Shakespeare in around 1606, during the reign of King James I. The play reflects many of the beliefs and concerns of Jacobean society, including the importance of loyalty to the monarch, the dangers of ambition, and the fear of the supernatural. King James was particularly interested in witchcraft, and Shakespeare included the three witches to appeal to the king and his audience. Many people at the time believed witches were real and capable of influencing events through evil magic.
The play also explores the concept of the Divine Right of Kings, the belief that monarchs were chosen by God and should not be challenged. Macbeth’s murder of King Duncan is therefore presented as both a political crime and a sin against God, leading to disorder throughout Scotland. Shakespeare may have been warning audiences about the consequences of disrupting the natural and social order. The character of Banquo is also portrayed positively because King James claimed to be descended from him, making the play partly a tribute to the ruling monarch.
