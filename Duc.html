My last duchess

Summary Quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

"My last duchess" is a dramatic monologue by Robert Browning that explores the theme of possessiveness and the dangers of unchecked power. The poem is narrated by the Duke of Ferrara, who describes his late wife and reveals his controlling nature and jealousy.

Quotes

"That's my last duchess painted on the wall"

"My gift of a nine-hundred-years-old name"

"I gave commands; Then all smiles stopped together."

"Notice Neptune, though, taming a sea-horse"

Analysed quotes

Quote: "That's my last duchess painted on the wall"
The possessive pronoun "my" shows his possessive nature and his ownership over her.
Similarly, subtle chremamorphism is used as he describes her to be "painted". This dehumanises her and reflects the patriarchal views within society at the time.
The adjective "last" further dehumanises her and reveals that he viewed her as a mere object for his disposal. It also implies that she was just the most recent, not the first or the last, showing how the cycle will repeat until the patriarchy is diluted.

Quote: "I gave commands; then all smiles stopped together."
This quote acts as a volta. It's a euphemism for the fact that he kills her and he uses advanced rhetoric and upper-class etiquette to hide his crimes.
The personal pronoun "I" presents him as a megalomaniac as he is so obsessed with power and status that he takes direct responsibility for her death.
The sibilance ("smiles", "stopped") creates a sinister tone. The soft sound may illustrate how he killed her discretely and still maintains his status.

Context

Browining was a Victorian poet writing here about the reneissance. He explores the power and control withing a patriarchal society.

Form and structure

A dramatic monologue is used, showing how the duke is overpowering as he does not let the guest speak.
The rhyming couplets (AABB e.g. "wall", "call", "hands", "stands") is unnatural for speech, showing his conniving nature and suggesting he has meticulously planned her murder too. The couplets could also imply him wanting to create an illusion of love and unity to uphold a facade of respectability.
His enjambment leaves no space for interruption, showing his arrogance, self-importance and obsessive nature.
