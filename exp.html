Summary

Wilfred Owen describes the suffering of soldiers in the trenches during World War I. Rather than fighting the enemy, they endure freezing weather, exhaustion, and constant fear. The poem highlights the harsh realities of war and suggests that nature is as deadly as the enemy itself.

Key quotes

"the merciless iced east winds that knive us..."

"Suddden successive flights of bullets streak the silence"

"But nothing happens"

"Less deadly than the air that shudders black with snow"

"Tonight, this frost will fasten on this mud and us"

Analysed quotes

Quote: "The merciless iced east winds that knive us..."
The personification of the winds through the adjective "merciless" shows how nature was almost (if not equally) as threatening as the human soldiers.
The collective pronoun "us" shows how this is the reality faced by all soldiers. Wilfred Owen reveals the reality behind the propaganda by showing that this is what all the soldiers truly faced.
The elipsis may create an elongated sense of suffering, showing how their pain is dragged out and they are constantly left in suspense.

Quote: "Tonight, this frost will fasten on this mud and us"
Fricative alliteration is used, perhaps to create an image of their shivering for the reader, evoking empathy.
Once again, the collective pronoun "us" is used, perhaps showing how the soldiers are united in their suffering.
The modal verb "will" is used to show certainty, depicting the repetitive nature of war. This suggests that war is futile, as the outcome can be predicted anyway.

Context

Wilfred Owen was a WW1 soldier who wanted to expose the truth about war and its futility. He was disgusted by the propaganda that made war seem honourable, therefore warning people about the reality through his poems.

Form and structure

The regular stanza lengths could mirror the uniformity of the military and their lack of freedom.
The frquent punctuation and end-stopped lines mimic the attacks from nature and the weather.
The repetition of "but nothing happens" at the end of stanzas creates anticlimactic endings that juxtapose the rest of the emotive stanza. Readers can empathize with the stress the soldiers felt while waiting for their certain death.
The pararhyme ("us" and "nervous") reflects the unease of the situation - mimicing how the soldiers are on edge.
