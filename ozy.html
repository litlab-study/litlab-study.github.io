Ozymandias

Summary Key quotes Analysed quotes Context Form and structure

Summary

Ozymandias is a poem by Percy Bysshe Shelley that explores the theme of the impermanence of power and the inevitable decline of all rulers and empires. The poem describes a traveler who comes across the ruins of a statue in the desert, with an inscription that reads, "My name is Ozymandias, king of kings: Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!" However, the surrounding landscape is barren and desolate, emphasizing the futility of Ozymandias's boastful claim to greatness.

Key quotes

"I met a traveller from an antique land"

"Two vast and trunkless legs of stone"

"Half sunk, a shattered visage lies"

"'Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!' Nothing beside remains."

"boundless and bare, the lone and level sands stretch far away."

Analysed quotes

Quote: "Half sunk, a shattered visage lies"
The phrase "half sunk" implies incompletedness - just like Ozymandias' legacy.
The adjective "sunk" suggests that the statue is being consumed by the sand, symbolising how time and nature erase even the greates human achievements.
The phrase "shattered visage" implies that Ozymandias' once-powerful image is now destroyed, showing that power and pride are fragile and temprorary.
This quote also uses hyperbaton (an inversion of word order), perhaps mirroring the inversion in world order when humans try to rule over nature.
This quote conveys the idea that no matter how powerful a leader is during their reign, with the passing of time they must eventually give way to wider, non-human forces like nature.
The sibilance ("sunk", "shattered") possibly reflects the wisper of the wind or sand - again linking to nature's power and building on the imagery to create a vivid picture.
There is also irony as the ruler wanted to be remembered forever, but all the remains is a faded, forgotten face. This may critique human hubris and the arrogance of rulers who think they can outlast time.

Quote: "'Look on my works, Ye Mighty, and despair!' Nothing beside remains"
The imperative "look" reflects his sense of superiority as he expects awe and submission from others. This may also further draw the reader into the poem, provoking reflection.
The formal, biblical title "Ye Mighty" shows his arrogance and hubris.
The possessive pronoun "my" reflects his egotism - everything great is credited to him despite his reliance on workers.
The strong emotional verb "despair" adds intensity, showing that Ozymandias wanted to provoke fear, not just admiration. This reveals a tyranical, oppressive mindset, fitting the poem's critique of rulers who abuse power.
This quote is also ironic - he expected his "work" to be an everlasting symbol of power, however, his statue is merely a ruin in an empty desert. Shelley uses this to highlight the transcient nature of human power, showing that even the greates achievements are eventually eroded by nature and time.
The surrounding emptiness and the line "nothing beside remains" completely undercuts his boast. This ironic contrast emphasises the poem's message that power is temprorary and that nature and time will eventually erase all human pride.

Quote: "Boundless and bare, the lone and level sands stretch far away"
The imagery paints a picture of the vast natural world that is indifferent to human accomplishment. This encapsulates the insignificance of human endeavours in the face of time and nature.
The adjective "lone" suggests isolation as there is no sign of Ozymandias' great empire. This emptiness may reflect the futility of his pride and vicious leadership.
The alliteration ("boundless", "bare", "lone", "level") draws the reader's attention and emphasises the vast emptiness surrounding the statue.
The desolate landscape creates a stark contrast to the supposed grand ststue of ozymandias. The poem ends with an image of the landscape, not Ozymandias, reinforcing that time and nature have the last word, not human rulers.

Context

Ozymandias was written in 1817 by Percy Bysshe Shelley, during a period of significant political upheaval in Europe. The poem reflects the Romantic interest in the sublime and the transient nature of human civilization. Shelley criticises absolute power and shows the ephemerality of oppressive human power.

Form and structure

A sonnet form is used. This may be ironic as Shelley shows no admiration for the ruler. Alternatively, it could mirror Ozymandias' excessive arrogance and love for himself. It's a mix of a petrarchan and shakespearean sonnet. These frequent changes may reflect how power moves between rulers and the inconsistency may mirror the unrest during his rule.
The iambic pentameter creates formality and shows the gravity of the subject.
Distance is created through the framed narrative, making it seem like a myth/legend. This could highlight the extent of his tyranny and impact on humans.
